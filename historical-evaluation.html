Forecast Accuracy — How well do we predict population exposure?

We compared three ways to correct archived 2025 exposure forecasts based on the Global Ensemble Forecast System (GEFS). Two methods use Google’s Population Dynamics Foundation Model (PDFM) to represent local geographic context.

Mean absolute error (MAE) measures how far forecasts were from observed exposure, in thousands of people per district-week. Lower is better.

Among 50 prioritized districts, climate-conditioned PDFM increased observed heat-exposure coverage from 18.05% to 21.03%, while identifying 36 heat events compared with 30 using conventional correction.

Research Findings — What do these results tell us?

PDFM’s forecasting gains varied by hazard and lead time. For 14-day persistent heat, adding PDFM directly gave the lowest exposure error. Climate-conditioned PDFM covered more exposed people and identified more heat events than direct augmentation. Forecast accuracy and district selection under a fixed review budget merit separate evaluation.

Agarwal et al. (2024) used PDFM’s geographic representations to improve downstream unemployment and poverty forecasts. Muslim et al. (2026) found gains for one-month dengue forecasts in Mexico and improved top-five cholera hotspot identification at four and eight weeks in the Democratic Republic of the Congo.
